## Concepts covered

**Option payoff structure.**

A European call option gives the holder the right (not obligation) to buy a stock at price K on date T. The payoff at expiry is:

```
max(S_T − K, 0)
```

Derived from first principles without prompting: if S_T > K, you buy at K and the stock is worth S_T — profit is S_T − K. If S_T ≤ K, you don't exercise — payoff is 0.

**The five BS inputs — all named and symbolised:**

| Symbol | Meaning | Direction effect on call price |
|--------|---------|-------------------------------|
| S | Current stock price | ↑ S → ↑ call (deeper in the money) |
| K | Strike price | ↑ K → ↓ call (more expensive to exercise) |
| r | Risk-free rate | ↑ r → ↑ call (discounting reduces PV of K) |
| σ | Volatility | ↑ σ → ↑ call (wider distribution, more upside) |
| T | Time to expiry | ↑ T → ↑ call (more time for stock to move) |

**Directional reasoning: r (the corrected error)**

Initial answer: ↑ r → ↑ call because interest rates increase the value of holding the stock.

Correction: ↑ r → ↑ call because the *discount factor* `e^(-rT)` shrinks, reducing the present value of the strike payment K that you'd make. A lower K-payment in present value terms means the call is worth more. Direction correct; mechanism needed one correction.

**d1 and d2 formulas — recalled from memory, unaided:**

```
d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)·T] / (σ·√T)
d2 = d1 − σ·√T
```

Both recalled exactly at end of session with no notes.

**Two-term formula structure:**

```
C = S·N(d1) − K·e^(−rT)·N(d2)
```

- `S·N(d1)`: expected receipt of the stock, weighted by a delta-adjusted probability
- `K·e^(−rT)·N(d2)`: present value of the strike payment, weighted by the probability the option finishes in the money

**Foundation chain understood:**

Log returns → Central Limit Theorem → log-normal distribution of S_T → normal distribution is the right mathematical tool for the N(d1), N(d2) terms.
