## New concept: MC convergence rate

**Key formula landed:** standard error of a sample mean = σ/√N

**Derivation path:**
- User connected σ/√N from W3 sample mean/variance work (unaided)
- Quadrupling N: 1/√(400,000) ÷ 1/√(100,000) = √(100,000)/√(400,000) = √(1/4) = 0.5 — derived correctly
- Implication: to cut error by 10×, need 100× more paths — correct unaided

**Initial misconception corrected:** user guessed "exponential" decrease (error < quarter at 4×N). Actual rate is 1/√N — slower than intuition suggests.

**Why this matters:** 10 extra decimal places = 10^20 more paths. Variance reduction (W13: antithetic variates) attacks σ directly instead of brute-forcing N.

- What they believed: "I would expect the error to decrease exponentially — less than a quarter of 0.01 since values converge at higher N."
- What was wrong: "Exponential" would mean each doubling cuts error by a constant ratio compounding very fast. The actual relationship is 1/√N — a square-root relationship, which is much slower. At 4×N, error halves (factor of 2), not quarters (factor of 4).
- What was said: Hint with direction — "think about the standard error of a sample mean; σ/√N is the formula you've seen in W3. What happens to 1/√N when N quadruples?" (Why this direction: the user knows σ/√N from sample mean estimation; naming that connection gives them a foothold without giving the answer.)
- Landing: Clean. User recalled σ/√N independently and derived 0.5 from the algebra.
